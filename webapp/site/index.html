02 — Why it matters

1/3

of people with epilepsy keep having seizures even on medication.

For many of them, surgery to remove the small patch of brain where seizures begin — the seizure onset zone — is the best hope. But finding that patch today means experts reading days of recordings by eye. It is slow, it varies from expert to expert, and only a few hospitals can do it.

03 — How it works

Brain waves in.
A map out.

Four steps, no medical degree required. The grey line under each step is for the technical folks.

/01

Listen

Small electrodes on the brain record its electrical activity — like dozens of microphones placed around a room.

iEEG · up to 64 channels

/02

Slice

The recording is cut into one-minute pieces that overlap, so nothing slips between the cracks.

60 s windows · 50% overlap

/03

Connect

Each electrode becomes a dot. Areas that rise and fall together get a line — thicker means more in sync.

Pearson-correlation graph · 18 features per node

/04

Judge

The AI looks at every dot together with its neighbours, listens hardest to the links that matter, and scores how likely it is to be the starting point.

Graph Attention Network · focal loss

      06 — Straight talk

      A second pair of eyes. Not the surgeon.

      • ✕Not an approved medical device — this is a university research project.
      • ✕It still misses about 1 in 3 onset electrodes, so it can’t replace an expert review.
      • ✓It suggests where to look first — doctors make every decision.
      • ✓Built on public data from several hospitals; it needs testing at new hospitals next.
